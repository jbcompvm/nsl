# Content Calendar & Publishing Cadence

**Status:** Draft — ready for operator review
**Owner:** Iris (Content & Marketing Lead)
**Purpose:** Sustainable content production that demonstrates expertise instead of claiming it

---

## Core Principles

1. **Show, don't claim** — Every piece demonstrates a capability rather than asserting one
2. **Specificity beats adjectives** — Numbers and examples, not "dramatically improves"
3. **Buyer's words, not ours** — Use language from Pythia's research
4. **One idea per piece** — A piece that argues three things persuades no one
5. **Distribution before production** — Decide where it goes before writing
6. **Owned over rented** — Every piece drives to something we own (assessment, resources)
7. **Honest scarcity** — We're small; say the true thing

---

## Content Types

### 1. Workflow Teardowns (Primary Asset)

**What:** Reference designs for workflows we build most often

**Structure:**
- The workflow name (e.g., "Inbound Lead Qualification")
- The trigger (what starts it)
- Every step, in order
- The tools involved
- The failure modes and what to do about each
- Rough build cost
- Rough ongoing cost by hand

**Frequency:** 1 per fortnight (biweekly)

**Why:** This is our strongest proof asset. It demonstrates thinking without fabricating client results.

**Examples in queue:**
1. Inbound Lead Qualification
2. New Client Intake
3. Proposal First Drafts
4. Overdue Invoice Chasing
5. Weekly Status Reporting

---

### 2. Assessment Teardowns (Secondary Asset)

**What:** Deep dives into how the assessment works — the scoring, the routing, the diagnosis logic

**Structure:**
- Why we measure what we measure
- How the four signals decide routing
- What each band means and why
- The logic behind the "not yet" answers

**Frequency:** 1 per month

**Why:** Shows rigor behind the free assessment. Most competitors won't explain their instruments; we'll explain ours.

---

### 3. "How We'd Approach This" Pieces (Tertiary Asset)

**What:** A real problem a prospect might have, and how we'd approach solving it

**Structure:**
- The problem (stated in buyer language)
- Why it's hard (the real constraints)
- How we'd approach it (step-by-step)
- What we'd exclude (what we'd say "no" to)
- Rough cost and timeline

**Frequency:** 1 per month

**Why:** Demonstrates reasoning without claiming a past result. "Here's how we'd think about your problem" instead of "we solved this for someone else."

**Examples in queue:**
1. "We've been experimenting with AI for months and nothing has changed" — how we'd diagnose the bottleneck
2. "We have four candidate workflows and no ranking" — how we'd structure the audit
3. "We need something live in under a month" — why we'd decline and what we'd suggest instead

---

### 4. Short Tips (Distribution-Only)

**What:** One-sentence or one-paragraph observations that teach something specific

**Structure:**
- The observation
- Why it matters
- One concrete example

**Frequency:** 1-2 per week (LinkedIn only)

**Why:** Maintains visibility on rented channels without requiring major production. Every tip links back to the assessment or a teardown.

**Examples:**
- "AI is an amplifier. Amplifying a broken process just produces faster chaos. Fix the process first, then automate."
- "The constraint isn't size; it's process clarity. A 10-person firm with a clear workflow beats a 100-person firm with a vague one."
- "You don't need a strategy deck. You need one workflow running in daily use. Start there."

## Weekly Cadence (Sustainable for One Person)

| Week | Content | Distribution | Time Estimate |
|------|---------|--------------|---------------|
| Week 1 | 1 Workflow Teardown | Website /resources/, LinkedIn | 6-8 hours |
| Week 2 | 2 Short Tips | LinkedIn only | 1-2 hours |
| Week 3 | 1 Workflow Teardown | Website /resources/, LinkedIn | 6-8 hours |
| Week 4 | 1 "How We'd Approach This" | Website /resources/, LinkedIn | 4-6 hours |

**Total:** ~17-24 hours per month

**Rationale:** This is what a one-person practice delivering 5-15 hours/week of client work can actually sustain. It's not daily; it's not hourly. It's biweekly teardowns with light weekly tips.

## Content Queue (First 3 Months)

### Month 1

**Week 1:** Workflow Teardown #1 — Inbound Lead Qualification
- Trigger: New lead arrives via website form
- Steps: Capture → Score → Route → Follow-up
- Tools: Form → CRM → Email
- Failure modes: Leads go cold, wrong person assigned, no follow-up
- Build cost: ~$3,000-4,500
- By hand cost: ~$800-1,200/month in lost opportunities

**Week 2:** Short Tip #1-2
- Tip 1: "95% of AI pilots fail for organizational reasons, not technical ones"
- Tip 2: "Start with one workflow, one metric, one shipped result"

**Week 3:** Workflow Teardown #2 — New Client Intake
- Trigger: Signed engagement letter
- Steps: Collect materials → Assign ID → Create workspace → Schedule kickoff
- Tools: Email → CRM → Document storage → Calendar
- Failure modes: Missing documents, unclear owner, delayed kickoff
- Build cost: ~$2,500-4,000
- By hand cost: ~$400-800/month in admin time

**Week 4:** "How We'd Approach This" #1
- Problem: "We've been experimenting with AI for months and nothing has changed"
- Approach: Diagnose the bottleneck → Name the missing piece → Build only after it's fixed

---

### Month 2

**Week 1:** Workflow Teardown #3 — Proposal First Drafts
- Trigger: RFP or opportunity identified
- Steps: Gather requirements → Pull templates → Generate draft → Review → Send
- Tools: CRM → Document generation → Email
- Failure modes: Inconsistent quality, missed deadlines, version confusion
- Build cost: ~$3,500-5,000
- By hand cost: ~$600-1,000/month in writer time

**Week 2:** Short Tip #3-4
- Tip 3: "The handoff gap — where strategy ends and implementation begins — is where most engagements fall apart"
- Tip 4: "You want a system that keeps running when the engagement ends"

**Week 3:** Workflow Teardown #4 — Overdue Invoice Chasing
- Trigger: Invoice past due date
- Steps: Detect → Generate reminder → Send → Escalate → Record payment
- Tools: Accounting software → Email → CRM
- Failure modes: Forgotten invoices, awkward conversations, inconsistent escalation
- Build cost: ~$2,000-3,500
- By hand cost: ~$300-600/month in collections time

**Week 4:** "How We'd Approach This" #2
- Problem: "We have four candidate workflows and no ranking"
- Approach: Time each by hand → Estimate build cost → Rank by ROI → Build the winner

---

### Month 3

**Week 1:** Workflow Teardown #5 — Weekly Status Reporting
- Trigger: End of week
- Steps: Gather updates → Format → Distribute → Archive
- Tools: Forms → Document generation → Email
- Failure modes: Inconsistent format, late submissions, no action on issues
- Build cost: ~$2,500-4,000
- By hand cost: ~$500-900/month in meeting time

**Week 2:** Short Tip #5-6
- Tip 5: "We're not the fastest option, and sometimes we're not the right one"
- Tip 6: "If you need something live in under a month, an automation freelancer is the right choice"

**Week 3:** Assessment Deep Dive — How the Scoring Works
- Why the four signals
- How bands are determined
- Why most firms score "not yet"
- What each band means for next steps

**Week 4:** "How We'd Approach This" #3
- Problem: "We need something live in under a month"
- Approach: Explain why we'd decline → Suggest freelancer alternative → Offer to review their build afterward

## Distribution Channels

### Owned Channels (Primary)

1. **Website /resources/** — All teardowns and deep dives published here first
2. **Email list** — Built through the assessment; new content sent to subscribers

### Rented Channels (Secondary)

1. **LinkedIn (operator's personal profile)** — Short tips and links to teardowns
   - No company page until we have a consistent posting rhythm
   - Every post links back to an owned page

2. **X/Twitter** — Optional, only if operator already active there
   - Same content as LinkedIn, reformatted

**No other channels** until the above are sustained for 3 months.

## Workflow for Producing Content

### Step 1: Topic Selection (15 minutes)
- Pick from the queue or propose a new topic
- Define the single idea the piece will argue
- Identify the primary CTA (always the assessment unless it's a teardown landing page)

### Step 2: Draft (2-4 hours for teardowns, 1-2 hours for tips)
- Write in the "show, don't claim" style
- Use verbatim buyer language from Pythia's research
- Include concrete numbers and examples
- End with proportionate CTA

### Step 3: Self-Audit (30 minutes)
- Check for fabricated results or claims
- Verify every number traces to a real source or is labeled as an estimate
- Ensure the skim path (headings + bold) carries the argument
- Confirm no jargon without explanation

### Step 4: Publish to /resources/ (15 minutes)
- Add to website
- Update the resources index page
- Set meta description and title

### Step 5: Distribute (15 minutes)
- Post to LinkedIn with a specific observation and link
- Optional: Add to any relevant communities (no spamming)

### Step 6: Log (5 minutes)
- Record in content log (see below)
- Note any feedback or questions received

## Success Metrics (Realistic for a Small Practice)

**Monthly targets:**
- 2-4 teardowns/deep dives published
- 4-8 short tips on LinkedIn
- 50-100 assessment completions (organic, not paid)
- 20-40 new email subscribers from content

**What we don't track:**
- Vanity metrics (likes, follows) unless they convert
- "Brand awareness" without a measurable path to assessment
- Any metric that doesn't tie to the assessment or a direct inquiry

---

## What We Don't Produce

1. **Fabricated case studies** — Ever. This is the single most fatal error for a new practice.
2. **Strategy-only content** — No "10 AI trends for 2027" without a concrete example
3. **Daily posting** — Unsustainable and leads to filler
4. **Multiple channels at once** — Focus on LinkedIn and owned content first
5. **Generic AI content** — "What is AI?" posts that add nothing
6. **Tool comparisons without context** — "n8n vs. Zapier" without a specific use case

---

## Contingencies

### If We Get Behind
- Skip a short tip week, not a teardown
- Teardowns are the core asset; tips are maintenance

### If a Teardown Takes Longer Than Expected
- Publish it as a "work in progress" with a clear "part 1 of 2" label
- Better to publish incomplete than not at all

### If the Operator Wants More Output
- Add a second teardown per month, not weekly
- Quality over quantity; one strong teardown beats three weak ones

### If a Piece Gets No Traction
- Diagnose: wrong topic, wrong distribution, or wrong timing?
- Don't abandon the cadence; adjust the topic

## Done Criteria

A content piece is done when:
- ✅ It demonstrates a capability rather than asserting one
- ✅ It names its distribution channel
- ✅ It links to the assessment (unless it's the assessment teardown itself)
- ✅ No `[NEEDS:]` markers remain unresolved
- ✅ Every claim traces to a real capability or cited fact

A cadence is done when:
- ✅ It's a schedule a one-person practice can actually sustain
- ✅ There's a topic queue for the next 6-8 weeks
- ✅ The workflow for producing content is documented
- ✅ Distribution channels are configured and tested
